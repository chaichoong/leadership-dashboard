## A blank number field is not zero

- **Airtable blank fields pass `!= 0` but fail `> 0`** — in an Airtable formula an empty number/currency field is NOT equal to 0, so `{blank} != 0` is TRUE while `{blank} > 0` is FALSE. Swapping `> 0` for `!= 0` to allow negatives makes every blank-field record take the wrong branch. This blanked `Report Amount` on 8,667 of 8,690 transactions in Jul 2026, taking out the P&L, dashboard, wealth and cashflow at once. To test "is this signed value set and non-zero" while keeping the blank fall-through, use **`ABS({field}) > 0`**. Whenever you change a formula condition, check it against a blank record, not just a populated one

## A GET by record id ignores the table

- **A read against the WRONG Airtable table returns 200 with full data** — `GET /v0/{base}/{table}/{recordId}` resolves the record id across the **whole base** and ignores the table in the URL. On 18 Sep 2026 three records were read, cross-checked, tabulated and queued for deletion as rows of `Tenancies` (`tblN51a88qTDB6iMH`); every read succeeded and every field was real. They actually lived in `tblCGmeUTyx1N7LNe` "Tenancies (Accounts Statement) Legacy", a dead 48-row table nothing reads. The **DELETE** is what exposed it, with `NOT_FOUND` on an id a `GET` had just returned in full. A write tells the truth; a read does not. To prove which table a record is in, LIST that table with pagination and look for the id, or attempt a write. This is the same family as the silent-zero rule below: Airtable answers `200 OK` to questions it has not actually been asked

## Never void a rental unit without checking every tenancy link

- **Never void a rental unit without checking every tenancy link on it** — the `airtable-tenancy-ender` skill set `Unit Status = Void` from the tenancy's own `Rental Unit` link with no check. On 18 Sep 2026, ending a former tenant's tenancy, that link was EMPTY on the live billing record while a legacy copy carried it, and the unit it pointed at was still let under the live tenancy on that unit. Voiding it would have taken out the occupancy rollups and the cash flow forecast, and nothing would have errored. Three rules, all of which cost a wrong turn to find. A unit's occupancy lives ONLY in its link fields, and in BOTH of them: `Tenancies` (`fldxOnUDg49C2PNVW`) and `Tenancies copy` (`fldmpIYp1cN0eQgWt`, which points into the legacy table). The plain-text `Tenants Field` (`fldUs1pONuxxL6Mcm`) is unmaintained and read the former tenant's name seventeen months after he left. And **the only reliable test of which tenancy a payment belongs to is the transaction's own `Tenancy` link, not the tenancy record that displays it** — legacy copies display transactions that point back at a different, often still-live, tenancy, which is how two live tenancies were nearly closed as past lets. The six-question gate is in the skill, mirrored at `.claude/skills/airtable-tenancy-ender/`

## Link fields, record ids and silent zeros (Airtable Conventions, 6 Aug 2026)

- **Filter linked records by record ID — but only a LOOKUP of record IDs, never the link field itself.** `ARRAYJOIN()` over a *link* field yields the linked record's **primary field (its display name)**, never its ID, so `FIND("recXXX", ARRAYJOIN({LinkField}))` matches nothing and returns `200 OK` with an empty list. Verified on Tasks `tblqB8b22hKBL4PF1`, 6 Aug 2026: `FIND("reca9ofzhuw13ZzGE", ARRAYJOIN({Business}))` → **0 rows**, while `ARRAYJOIN({Business})="Operations Director"` and `FIND("rec4b5MDoaxEC7WRE", ARRAYJOIN({Record ID (Used for Automation) (from Team Members)}))` both match. So:
  - **Preferred:** add or use a lookup field that surfaces the linked record's `RECORD_ID()`, then `FIND()` against that. Stable across renames. This is what the rule has always meant
  - **Acceptable when no such lookup exists:** match the display name (`{Business}="Operations Director"`). Works today, but breaks silently the day someone renames the record, so pair it with a control
- **Every filterByFormula that counts something needs an expected count.** A wrong field name, a link-field ID match, or a bare date comparison all return `200 OK` and `{"records":[]}` — a broken query and a genuinely empty result are indistinguishable. State the number you expect and fail loudly on a miss. Date fields are the sharpest edge: `{Date}="2026-08-06"` returns zero even when the record exists; use `DATESTR({Date})="2026-08-06"` or `IS_SAME({Date},DATETIME_PARSE(...),'day')`. A silent zero on an existence check that gates a create writes the duplicate the check exists to prevent

## A master switch that reads On is not the setting that earns (Content Engine, 20 Sep 2026)

Moved from CLAUDE.md "Known Anti-Patterns" on 21 Sep 2026, word for word. CLAUDE.md keeps the three rules.

- **A master switch that reads On is not the setting that earns, and a filter on HOW something was uploaded hides it from the report that would have caught it** — the Content Engine switched every YouTube video's monetisation On and every surface agreed: the morning line read "content monetisation: every YouTube episode and Short On". On 20 Sep 2026 Kevin said the long episodes were not earning. He was right. The master switch buys a pre-roll; **mid-roll ads were off on 817 of the 888 videos over 8 minutes**, with YouTube's own break point already computed and waiting on 695 of them, and a further 923 videos from 2020 to Nov 2023 had no video ads at all, only display banners. Nothing was broken, nothing errored, and the thing being measured was simply not the thing that makes money. Three rules came out of it. **Measure the setting that produces the outcome, not the switch nearest to it.** **A selector on provenance is a selector on visibility** — `monetise_long_video` matched posts with `route == "api"`, so every GoHighLevel upload (which carries GHL's own post id, not a YouTube one) was stepped over in silence, and the report used the same filter, so 2054's episode, 2054's Short and 2195's episode were invisible in both places; match on the identity of the thing (the video id, resolved through the episode's `youtube_link`), never on how it arrived, and report what you cannot resolve. And **a 200 from an undocumented internal endpoint is not a write**: Studio's `metadata_update` needs the page's minted BotGuard `attestationResponseData`, and replayed without it it answers `200 OK` and changes nothing — proved on `Rs8xHbD5miQ`, 200 then `hasMidrollAds` still false on read-back. Same family as the Airtable silent-zero rule above. So a genuine UI save mints an attestation, the rest of the batch replays it, and **every id is read back off the source before anything is called done**. Guarded by `tests/content-engine-ads.test.js` (back-tested: restoring the route filter fails "does not filter YouTube posts by upload route")

## The open page is not the saved record (Adobe e-sign, 23 Sep 2026)

- **A read-back on the page you just changed proves the page, not the record** — `scripts/adobe-assign.js` moved a tenancy agreement's landlord date box to Agile Lets, read it back as Agile Lets' colour, and reported ok. Kevin opened the draft and found the box still on the tenant. The read was true: the open page had the move. Adobe had not saved it. Adobe saves a draft about 8 to 11 seconds after changes go quiet (a PUT to the document's asset, 204), and the read-back clicked a box every 3 seconds straight after the last move, then closed the browser. Reproduced twice on a test draft: the page showed the box on one person, the reopened draft on the other, and Adobe raised a "leave this page?" prompt only when a change was unsaved. So the script now waits for that save, reloads the draft, and proves every signature and date box from the saved copy by colour (each box carries its owner's colour, each recipient row the same), refusing any box it cannot prove. Same family as "a 200 is not a write" above: **check the thing that will be used, after it has been stored.** Guarded by `tests/adobe-assign.test.js` (back-tested: letting the verdict accept a wrong owner fails the 23 Sep case)

## A rule written for sessions reached the robots, and went out in public copy (Content Engine, 24 Sep 2026)

- **A headless writer whose output is published word for word must run with no session rules at all** — the 21 Sep 2026 set-up clean-up kept the close-out rule on for headless runs on purpose (the task robots benefit from it). The Content Engine's copywriter is also a headless run, and it obeyed: from 21 Sep it ended its replies with "CLOSE-OUT ... Outstanding: none ... Safe to close? Yes". The copy parser takes each section up to the next heading, so the block rode on the LAST section of every reply, the Podcast Copy and the YouTube Reels Copy, of episodes 2066 to 2071. It went out on four public YouTube Shorts and three Spotify episodes, and nothing flagged it: the rules check looks for em dashes and figures, not for prose that is not copy. Worse, when the Stop hook demanded a second turn the run died on `--max-turns 1`, so 2069's teaser copy was never written, its teasers never posted, and the nightly job's `|| exit 1` on the copy step ended the night before cards, publishing and the report. Proved by one prompt run twice: hooks on ends "Safe to close? Yes", `--settings '{"disableAllHooks": true}'` comes back clean. Four rules. **Anything a model writes for the public runs with `disableAllHooks`.** **Copy is refused, not trusted:** a reply is cut at the top of a trailing session block, a field still holding one is never written, the approval card is blocked and the publisher refuses every stage. **One failed step never ends the night**, and a record left half-written is retried. **Look at what went out, not at what was meant to** — the leak was found by reading the Spotify upload's failure screenshot, not by any check. Guarded by the platform_copy, approval and publish selftests (each back-tested by removing the fix). In the same session: 38 GB of finished working copies had refused the next pull 1 GB short (`watch.clear_leftovers`), and a recording in two parts was rendered as two episodes, the second over the first (render.py joins a part to the part before it; a second long recording that is not a part is never rendered over the episode)
