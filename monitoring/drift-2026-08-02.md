### DEAD select choices — 3, and why nine months of reports missed them

| Constant | ID | Was |

The field's live choices are **In Payment, Overdue, Paused, Inactive**. Checked against all 180 cost
records: In Payment 81, Inactive 62, Paused 28, Overdue 9. Nothing holds a removed value, because a
removed choice cannot be held.

**Runtime impact today: none.** Only the three `PS.ten*` constants are ever read
([js/income.js:75](js/income.js:75)); the eight cost constants are declared and never used. That is
luck rather than design, and it is exactly what makes this worth writing down.

**The check that should have caught this could never have caught it.** The task spec says the
snapshot is `{tableId: {name, fields: {fieldId: {name, type}}}}` — there is no room in that
structure for a choice ID. Every report from 2026-07-30 to yesterday states that all "11
select-choice IDs ... resolve against today's snapshot". That sentence cannot be true of any
select-choice ID, dead or alive, because the snapshot has never held one. It was a control that
matched nothing, reported forever as a pass — the failure mode CLAUDE.md names in
"A running job is not a working job".

Fixed two ways this run:
1. Today's snapshot now stores a `choiceIds` list for every select field — **2,023 IDs**. The
   dead-choice check runs against the snapshot and fails on a removal. Back-tested by resolving the
   three removed IDs against the new snapshot: all three come back dead, all 10 live ones resolve.
   **IDs only, no labels — see §5.**
2. `reference-map.json` drops the 3 dead IDs and picks up the two live ones config.js never
   declared: `costPaused` (`selzQhQoQQXe3DXMK`) and `costInactive` (`sel5UTLLcZTdRVq6m`) — between
   them **half the Costs table**, 90 of 180 records.

Re-verified against the snapshot alone: **422/422 fields, 35/35 tables, 19/19 records,
10/10 select choices.**

### The measurement was wrong, and it was hiding the worst cases

Version gaps have been computed by comparing `pageVer` and `sopVer` as **decimal numbers**. They are
not decimals. `1.30` is major 1, minor 30 — twenty-four versions ahead of `1.6`. As a float, `1.30`

| invoices | 2.18 | 2.2 | "in sync" | **16** |
| sitemap | 1.19 | 1.2 | "in sync" | **17** |

Yesterday's report went the wrong way on this: it took the earlier figure of 23, recomputed it "on a

**The product was never wrong.** [js/sitemap.js:624](js/sitemap.js:624) compares the two as strings
(`p.pageVer !== p.sopVer && p.sopFile`) and has been counting all five correctly the whole time.
This is a defect in the daily report, not in the platform.
