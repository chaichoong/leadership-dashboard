Four findings were identified and **none were filed**. The queue refused this run at the filing

means *stop, do nothing else*. That instruction was honoured. The findings are written up here so
they are not lost, and need re-filing on the next healthy run:

1. **HIGH — a routine that loses its lease keeps running and is never told.**
   `scripts/job-queue.py`. This run acquired at 00:04:00Z with a 90-minute lease, the Mac slept,
   and the lock was force-broken at 04:58:15Z after 17,654 seconds held. The routine carried on
   for three more hours believing it held the machine. The only reason it found out was an
   incidental `heartbeat` call; nothing raised an error, and there is no check between the
   acquire and the work. A routine permitted to write code would have gone on writing. **Fix:**
   have the long-running steps re-assert the lock, and make a broken lease abort the run rather
   than being discoverable only on request.

2. **HIGH — the queue served a dead holder for nearly five hours.**
   Same event log: `audiobook-backfill` was refused at 00:04:22Z by a lock whose owner was
   already asleep, and the lock was not reclaimed until 04:58. A 90-minute lease held for 294
   minutes is the failure. **Fix:** reclaim on lease expiry rather than on the next contender's
   arrival, and log a `lease-exceeded` event when a holder outlives its lease.

3. **MEDIUM — CHECK 4 is structurally incapable of passing.**
   The preview browser has no PAT, so four of six health checks have measured an empty page for
   two runs running. **Fix:** either seed the preview with a fixture dataset so the checks have
   something to assert against, or delete them and stop reporting a check that cannot fail.

4. **LOW — `monitoring/reference-map.json` lags `js/config.js` by 22 field IDs.**
   All 22 resolve; nothing is broken. **Fix:** regenerate the map.
