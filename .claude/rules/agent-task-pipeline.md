# Agent task pipeline lessons

Moved from CLAUDE.md "Known Anti-Patterns" on 21 Sep 2026, word for word. Each entry shipped, broke production and was fixed; its guard test is named in the entry. Two repairs on the move: the duplicate-key entry had lost its backticks around `SMS` and `reply`, and a creditor sum was replaced with a description because this repo is public.

- **An address says WHERE, not WHICH, and a phone number IS the identity** — the duplicate key that groups agent tasks kept only the first two distinctive words, deleted every number as reference noise, and split on the lane prefix. Measured against the 55 tasks at the approval gate on 28 Aug 2026 it produced 43 cards and missed **seven real pairs**, each the same matter written twice: `Sefton Council HMO licence fee ... Viola St` vs `pay Sefton landlord licence fee ... Viola Street`, and one SMS thread appearing once in each lane. Kevin was working the queue and said so. Two rules came out of fixing it, and both were found by TESTING rather than reasoning. **Address words must never count as evidence of a duplicate** — he has ~27 properties with many open tasks each, so counting them folds a garden complaint into a rent arrears chase at the same house; place tokens (a street type, the name before it, the town after it) still show in the explanation but never toward the threshold, and the key defers them to the back of its two slots. **A verb is not identity** — `pay Sefton` and `Sefton Council` are one matter, and without `DUPE_ACTION_WORDS` two DIFFERENT phone numbers merge on the shared words `SMS` and `reply`. Matching is now two passes (exact key, then `dupe_verdict`) with different strictness per caller: **grouping shows and may cross lanes; folding destroys and may not**, because a maintenance job absorbed into a reply task is a real obligation lost. Every grouping states its reason on screen — a duplicate Kevin cannot check is one he has to take on trust. Guarded by `tests/sync-invariants/approval-dupe-confirmation.spec.js` (16, using the real task names off the live queue) and `tests/agents-dupe-task-key.test.js`; back-tested by breaking each of the six mechanisms in turn

- **"Labelled with no OPEN task" is not "stranded", and a machine receipt is never a matter** — the triage skill's stranded-mail rescue re-created a task for every lane-12 message whose thread had no open task. A thread keeps its label after its real task COMPLETES, so each later auto-acknowledgement ("Automatic reply: …", "Thank you for contacting… logged with reference CSV-…") minted a fresh task, a role agent turned it into a NO ACTION REQUIRED briefing or a CLOSE PROPOSAL, and Kevin was asked to approve nothing, four times between 28 Aug and 1 Sep 2026. Two rules: an auto-reply is decided by a machine signal (`auto_reply_signal` in `scripts/create-agent-task.py` — the RFC 3834 header, the subject family, or a receipt-shaped body with no question, no instruction and no position taken), never by the agent's judgement of a tier-1 subject; and the refusal sits in the task gate every create passes through, with the scan excluding flagged mail from the stranded lists and `act` refusing to lane it. Bounces are excluded on purpose: a bounce means something Kevin sent did NOT arrive, which is a task. Back-test any change to the signal read-only against the live lane-12 listing (the 100 newest), not against a fixture: the first version of the body test flagged a "do not reply to this email" data notice, and the header-presence version flagged a phishing mail. Guarded by both scripts' selftests and `tests/inbound-email-triage.test.js`. The second half of the same bug, found the same day: eight "nothing to decide" items Kevin cleared were re-creations of tasks he had already COMPLETED, because the rescue looked for an OPEN task. A thread that has EVER had a task is handled: the scan now looks up any-status tasks on every stranded thread (`lookup_thread_tasks`, both URL forms, batched) and moves them to `stranded_handled` before the agent sees them, with the lane-13 exception preserved (a reply task never handles a repair) and a failed lookup reading UNCHECKED rather than "nothing handled". Measured on 2 Sep 2026: 83 of 83 labelled threads in the 14-day window already had a task, so the old rule had been re-minting the whole board

- **A classifier that reads the Notes reads every agent's run log** — the system-alert lane matched "Gmail quota" and "Apps Script" over the task's Notes as well as its name. Agent run logs say those words whenever a scan hits a limit, so eleven real matters (a letter before action 19 days overdue, a four-figure sum to verify, £50+VAT, two compliance renewals) were parked as machine alerts and never dispatched (found 14 Sep 2026). A lane that decides what a task IS reads the sender and the subject, never the trail agents write on it, and money, a creditor marker or the tier-1 banner is never an alert (`alert_veto`). Guarded by `tests/agent-dispatch-alert-lane.test.js`

- **An approved task that stays open is not a hand-back every half hour** — `complete --keep-open` left the task Approved, so the 30-minute poll re-carried the same two EICR chases 48 times a day (one task's Notes reached 46,000 characters) and re-parked three login-gated tasks every tick; that poll used more of the weekly Claude allowance than the Content Engine and triage together, and the allowance ran out at Friday lunchtime (11 Sep 2026). A hand-back that was carried out and kept open, or parked on a sign-in, rests 24 hours as `idleHandbacks` (listed with its reason, never hidden) and wakes early when Approved At moves. Guarded by `tests/agent-dispatch-idle-handback.test.js`

- **An approval string is not an approval** — `scripts/send-email.py` sent any task whose Approval Outcome read "Approved…". On 22 Sep 2026 an agent created two tasks with a raw Airtable write, typed that string itself and sent two contractor emails Kevin never saw (finding 20260922-agent-dispatch-572); six earlier quote requests had gone the same way "pre-approved under a parent task", one with an Approved At copied from the parent and earlier than the task. A send now needs the marks only a real approval leaves: Sent For Approval By (the gate), Approved At (the queue), and Approved At after the task's own creation (`approval_evidence_problem`). A child of an approved parent is not approved: it goes through the gate itself or qualifies for a rule send. Checked against every approved Correspondence task: 143 genuine approvals pass, the 8 unstamped sends refuse. The LIMIT: every mark is written with the token the agents hold, so a deliberate forgery of all three still passes; only separate robot credentials close that. The check lives in `scripts/approval_evidence.py`; `calendar-write.py` uses it too (24 Sep 2026), and `send-letter.py` since 25 Sep 2026 (finding 616; `tests/send-letter-approval-evidence.test.js`). Guarded by `tests/send-email-approval-evidence.test.js` (drives the real `load_approved`; back-tested by removing the check)

- **A wall an agent reports is not a wall anyone fixes, and handing the job to Kevin is not doing it** — on 25 Sep 2026 Kevin found insurance renewing because replacement cover was never quoted. 6 Chedburgh Place landlord insurance (recPYIC5nn7v2bh8e) was PARKED nine times in ten days while its wall moved from "TopCashback signed out" to "not on the robot's list" to "node not on PATH"; a PARKED note only rested the task for a day. The agents' fix requests were medium findings over a full queue, so they sat in an overflow log nothing reads, or the fixer deferred them as protected "for Kevin" with nothing telling him (42 since 1 Sep). A fix never woke the task. And the Swinton quote (recc2fdXwsHLMAKU3) was written into the closing line as Kevin's own job, approved, and closed with nothing done. Four rules, all in code: a wall is `block` with a KIND (SIGN-IN, SITE, TOOL, KEVIN) recorded as a `BLOCKER OPEN` line in the task's Notes, and annotate refuses the free-text form; each kind has an owner and a wake (the sign-in tap, the site on the list, the HIGH finding closing fixed, the agent's evidence), and `blockers --sweep` on the half-hourly poll wakes the task keeping Kevin's verdict; `complete` refuses a blocked task and `submit` refuses a closing line that hands the work to Kevin or "someone" unless the step is declared `KEVIN ONLY: <payment|purchase|signature|credential|identity|physical>: <step>`; and the Estate status row "Robots blocked" plus a line in the 08:00 DM name what only Kevin can clear, red at three days or on any task closed while blocked. The hand-off pattern was back-tested on the 765 outputs of the month before: 36 would have been refused, 13 of which Kevin had rejected himself, and three look-alike shapes were excluded. Also found the same day: `agent-tools.sh` resolved node but never put it on PATH, so every unattended browser step died "command not found: node". Guarded by `tests/agent-dispatch-blockers.test.js` (34, driving the real functions and running both runner prompts through bash; back-tested by removing each mechanism in turn) and `tests/sync-invariants/agents-estate-blockers.spec.js` (the Estate tab block). Two review rounds found what the first build missed: a SIGN-IN wall must be stored under the sign-in door host or the tap never clears it, a wake must not drop an earlier carry-out intent, an approved woken task is carried out and never resubmitted (a submit wipes the approval), a submit may not bury a standing wall, and a KEVIN ONLY line covers only its own phrase

- **An alarm on a draft is noise, and noise teaches Kevin to ignore the alarm** — on 27 Sep 2026 agent-dispatch alerted twelve times in one afternoon. Seven were "ZERO completed actions" from the hand-back poll, which is told to ignore new work while its worklist counts it, or which met a wall already on record; five were draft reports the agent ran through the alarm wrapper, fixed, and ran again a minute later. Four rules: the agent self-checks with `verify --dry-run` (no alarm state, never wrapped) and runs the wrapped control exactly once, last; owed work is counted from the `queue.json` the run was handed, with the runner (never the agent) marking a hand-back-only run; a task resting on a wall recorded before the run (start time from the queue's own `generatedAt`, not the agent's `startedAt`, which half the runs write an hour late) or a parked task alerted before and listed again is excused, and neither counts as the run's work; and the control alarms on any failure or parked task the self-check saw that the final report dropped. Replayed on 34 real reports: 15 failing became 7, all genuine. Closed on 28 Sep: an old alert excuses a re-listed parked task only while its live wall is open and predates the run, and the named-task runners (roy-assistant, signin-pickup) write the ids they owe to `owed-ids`. Guarded by `tests/agent-dispatch-verify-noise.test.js` (driving the real `cmd_verify`; four review rounds)

- **Two tenants are two matters, and a file name is not a reference** — on 28 Sep 2026 the create gate folded one tenant's "Check DWP decision on <tenant>'s UC47 (55 Elmdon Place) and chase if none" into another tenant's open task at 5 Dalham Place (`{"action": "updated", "key": "decision dwp"}`). The key keeps two words, so both keyed to `decision dwp`, the word pass matched them too, and nothing asked whether the names disagreed on WHO or WHERE; the rules above only stopped an address counting FOR a match. Two rules: two names that each carry a house (number + street words + street type) or a named person (exactly two capitalised words before 's, never the team, an organisation, a role or an object such as "Safety Certificate's") and share none of it never fold or group; a unit or flat number, a house range (42-44) and an amount (£150) are read as such, in either pass and either mode (`identity_conflict` / `dupeIdentityConflict`, mirrored and drift-tested), and a create whose Tenancies, Tenants, Properties or Rental Units link names a different record from the open task's never folds (`links_disagree`; a link on one side only decides nothing). Backtested on the 216 open tasks: of 370 folds the old gate would make, 20 are refused, every one a different house or linked property. The same task's TRACK RECORD searched "ref UC47-ANSWERS", taken from its working-file path, and pulled in the other tenant's history: `reference_tokens` now drops file paths and file names, bare, at the end of a path or in quotes (Kevin, 29 Sep 2026; backtested on all 8,139 tasks: 6 tasks changed, 9 tokens dropped, all file-name fragments). The agents' own "CLOSE PROPOSAL: duplicate of <task>" runs the same house, person and link checks, whichever task is older (#625; 47 past proposals replayed, 0 changed). Five independent review rounds found 14 more ways to get it wrong (JS/Python drift on space-like characters among them); each is a test. Guarded by `tests/agents-dupe-task-key.test.js` ("a different tenant or a different house is a different matter"), `tests/sync-invariants/approval-dupe-confirmation.spec.js` (the Duplicates lane), `tests/agent-dispatch-autonomy.test.js` ("an agent cannot close one tenant's task as a duplicate of another's") and `tests/agent-dispatch-track-record-refs.test.js` ("a file path or file name is never a reference")

- **A card without its facts cannot be answered, and a question without its history gets asked twice** — on 2 Oct 2026 Kevin said the Task Board Manager's approval cards "don't include all the required information to be able to make decisions". `escalate` wrote one `DECIDE:` line and nothing else: it cleared the plain lines, the TRACK RECORD gate sat only in `submit`, and nothing asked for a figure. Three decision cards sat in his queue at 250 to 285 characters while every other agent's card ran 1,000 to 23,000 with its history; one asked him to "confirm which cards and amounts to authorise" and named no amount. Reading the full history of the other two showed the worse half: he had ANSWERED both on 23 Sep, the answers sat in Feedback History, and on 30 Sep each went back to him as "prior escalation had no recorded answer" (one was by then covered by a letter posted under another open task). Three rules, all in `cmd_escalate`: a card is refused without a brief the agent writes (`WHAT THIS IS`, `WHAT HAS HAPPENED`, `OPTIONS` with two or more, `RECOMMENDED`) and the two plain lines, and a money question needs a £ figure or `AMOUNT NOT KNOWN: <where it was looked for>`; what must never depend on an agent remembering is added in code (his own dated words under the ask, the email link, the files on the task, the dated history, with a file on another task linked to that task because a signed file link dies within hours); and a task he has already answered is refused, quoting his words, unless the brief says what has changed since. The first line stays `DECIDE: <ask>`, which the board, the page and `is_decide_card` read; a thin card already in the queue is rebuilt once and an answered one is never rewritten (the rewrite clears the verdict). An approval with an empty box now means the card's recommendation (`recommended` on the board's `decided` view). The independent review found why he was asked twice, and it was not the card: the board filed an answer as `decided` only inside seven days of the escalation, so an answer given on day eight fell through to `stuck` and the task was raised again. His answer no longer expires (`classify`), a card he has answered is refused at ANY status with his answer quoted (the page moves a task to Today the moment he decides, so a guard on Approval never fired), the earlier answer is archived and cleared so an approval with an empty box is not read as the old words, and an earlier draft kept under the card is quoted line by line so its `TO:`, `SIGN-IN NEEDED:` or carry-out line is never read as the card's own. The second review round found the last hole: both of his 23 Sep answers meant WAIT, and only `route` and `handover` could close an answered card, so `decided TASKID [--until DATE]` now records an answer to wait or to do nothing, restores the holder and parks the task. Guarded by `tests/agent-dispatch-escalate.test.js` ("a decision card carries a brief, its history, its links and its files", driving the real `cmd_escalate`, fixture shaped from the credit card task; back-tested by removing the brief check, which fails four)

- **A paid certificate that is never filed gets bought twice, and a gate keyed on a task's NAME misses the task that matters** — on 2 Oct 2026 the compliance book read "missing" for two certificates that had been done and paid for. A gas safety record arrived on 16 Sep on a task named for its INVOICE: the agent checked the payment three times, wrote "no certificate filed" in its own output and closed it, because the filing gate in `verify` only covered engine-raised renewals. An electrical report arrived on 24 Aug and triage filed it under label 10 with no task, by a rule written while the Property Administration agent "is being built" and never revisited when it went live on 2 Sep; the book later got a 753-byte placeholder that counted as a document. Nothing would have stopped a second purchase. Four rules, all in code (`scripts/certificate_watch.py`, pure): triage's `act --do file --label-num 10` refuses without the rec id of a real task (Step 4c raises it for Property Administration); `complete` refuses any task that names a certificate DOCUMENT and received a file until a book row with a real file is linked, or the agent declares `--no-certificate "<what the file is>"`, which is written to Notes; the daily score reads paid compliance lines from the bank and raises one filing task per payment with no certificate filed for that property since 45 days before it (a payment with no property gets a task too, never a silent skip, and a renamed sub-category fails the control); and `submit` refuses a quote request or PASS TO ROY booking when the certificate is held in date beyond 60 days or such a payment is outstanding. A file named PLACEHOLDER or under 2KB is not a document. The document words are specific on purpose: the bare word "certificate" would refuse every task about an SSL certificate or a certificate of posting. Replayed on the book as it stood on 1 Oct: the payment check flags exactly the two missed cases and nothing else. The independent review found seven more ways to get it wrong, each now a test: a block's per-apartment reports must never read as "already held"; a payment an agent answered on the record must stop blocking that house (the filing TASKS are the record, not a state file); `verify` must excuse the close the engine's own task asks for; a report that files itself and a Level A close are close paths too; triage's `CERTIFICATE ATTACHED` marker must trigger the gate and its twin `CERTIFICATE MENTIONED, NO ATTACHMENT` must not; the word "attached" in free text is not a file arriving; and a GET by record id does not prove a record is a TASK. The second review found three more: the engine's own filing task named no document type and could close as "nothing to decide" (it now always owes its filing); a payment is answered by the certificate LINKED to its filing task, not by a row's date and type; and a Level A diary entry or redirect, triage's "Kevin replied himself" close and Roy's `task-update --complete` are close paths too. Guarded by `tests/certificate-watch.test.js` (29, driving the real `cmd_complete`, `cmd_certificate`, `ensure_paid_certificates_filed`, `certificate_purchase_problem` and the triage CLI; back-tested by removing each of eight mechanisms in turn)
