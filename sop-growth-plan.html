Real Estate Growth Plan

How the page finds money in the portfolio, what it writes to Airtable, and how to work it.
SOP v1.0 · page v1.0 · 9 September 2026

In one line

Open the page, do the single action in the green box, then the next row down. Fill any "fact needed" as you learn it. Nothing on the page contacts a tenant or a council; the task you create is the send.

1. Why the page exists

Universal Credit pays a single claimant aged 35 or over the 1-bed Local Housing Allowance rate even when they rent one room in a shared house (Universal Credit Regulations 2013, Schedule 4, paragraph 2B). Housing Benefit does not, which is why the portfolio used to give an over-35 two rooms. In Haverhill the difference is £526.33 a month (room rate) against £900.00 (1-bed rate). The page reads every tenant, unit and property in Airtable and prices every lever that follows from that rule, plus council tax, voids and head-lease take-backs, then ranks them by money per unit of effort.

3. The benefit cap, and why every rent is "cap-safe" first

The cap for a single adult outside London is £1,229.42 a month (2026-27). A single over-35 on the standard allowance of £424.90 plus £900 housing costs is £95.48 over it, and pays that shortfall from their own allowance. So the page prices every uplift and new let at the safe rent (£804.52 in Haverhill) unless the tenant's record says why the cap does not apply. The full rate appears in the "If exempt" column.

  • Exemptions that lift the cap: LCWRA element, PIP or DLA in payment, a carer element, or household earnings of £881+ a month.
  • Record it on the tenant (property card, "cap" dropdown) once you have seen the UC statement or award letter. The plan re-prices at once.
  • Use the calculator for anyone new: household, age, rent, LCWRA, earnings. If it says capped, the rent is the safe rent, from day one.
Housing Benefit claimants keep the shared rate in a shared house at any age. The page never uplifts an HB tenant in a room.

4. Working the page

  1. Next single action (green box): do it, or click "Create the task".
  2. Facts the plan still needs: dates of birth, cap exemptions, lettable rooms, council tax payer. Each one writes straight to Airtable and re-prices the plan.
  3. Strategic order: click a row for the evidence and the first step. Adopt records it in the Growth Plan table. Task creates an Airtable task (paper and legal to Kevin, works and lettings to Roy) and links it to the row. Done and Drop take it out of the totals; "Show done and dropped" brings it back.
  4. Property cards: rooms in use against lettable rooms, each tenant's rent against their rate, and the three fields the plan needs per house.
  5. Joint tenancies: the candidate houses and a draft clause. The clause is a draft for the AI Legal and Compliance head to check before it goes in an agreement.
  6. Finding tenants: where the last 254 applicants came from and what each channel is worth now.
  7. Assumptions: every figure not read from a live record. Edit here; it saves to Airtable. Update the LHA and benefit rows each April.

6. Health checks

  • Portfolio tables fetched: fails on a zero count in properties, units, tenants or tenancies.
  • Settings rows present: fails under 10 rows (the page would be running on built-in defaults).
  • LHA table in date: warns after 31 March 2027.
  • Every property has a postcode: a house with no postcode gets no LHA rate.
  • Ages known for UC room tenants and Benefit cap exemptions recorded: the two facts that move the paper-trail total most.
  • Plan rows match a live lever: a Growth Plan row whose key no longer exists (tenant moved).
  • Totals reconcile: the actionable figure equals its four buckets.
