Real Estate Growth Plan

How the page finds money in the portfolio, what it writes to Airtable, and how to work it.
SOP v1.5 · page v1.5 · 9 September 2026

In one line

Open the page, do the single action in the green box, then the next row down. Fill any "fact needed" as you learn it. Nothing on the page contacts a tenant or a council; the task you create is the send.

1. Why the page exists

Universal Credit pays a single claimant aged 35 or over the 1-bed Local Housing Allowance rate even when they rent one room in a shared house (Universal Credit Regulations 2013, Schedule 4, paragraph 2B). Housing Benefit does not, which is why the portfolio used to give an over-35 two rooms. In Haverhill the difference is £524.90 a month (room rate, £121.13 a week) against £897.52 (1-bed rate, £207.12 a week), read from LHA Direct in September 2026. The page reads every tenant, unit and property in Airtable and prices every lever that follows from that rule, plus council tax, voids and head-lease take-backs, then ranks them by money per unit of effort.

3. The benefit cap, and the CRF Housing Payment

The cap for a single adult outside London is £1,229.42 a month (2026-27). A single over-35 on the standard allowance of £424.90 plus £897.52 housing costs is £93.00 over it. The housing element paid to the landlord is what gets cut; the tenant keeps their standard allowance. Kevin's ruling (9 September 2026): the rent is always the full LHA rate, and the shortfall is applied for as a Crisis and Resilience Fund Housing Payment from Anglia Revenues Partnership (the payment that replaced Discretionary Housing Payments on 1 April 2026), with the payment option set to landlord. The "CRF a month" column is that figure.

  • Exemptions that lift the cap: LCWRA element, PIP or DLA in payment, a carer element, or household earnings of £881+ a month. Record it on the tenant (property card, "cap" dropdown) once seen on the UC statement; the CRF figure drops to nil.
  • CRF awards are short term or one-off. Diarise the renewal; the shortfall returns when an award ends.
  • Use the calculator for anyone new: household, age, rent, LCWRA, earnings. It gives the CRF amount to put on the form.
Housing Benefit claimants keep the shared rate in a shared house at any age. The page never uplifts an HB tenant in a room.

4. Working the page

  1. Metric cards: click any card to see the rows that make it up, with the same figures the card adds up.
  2. Missing dates of birth sit on the blocked tenant inside their property pack: enter the date, or confirm 35 or over, and the tenant joins the plan.
  3. The work, property by property: one pack per property, in the order to do them, so a single visit closes a whole house. Open a pack for what to take (Before you go), each tenant with what they sign, what to collect from them and what gets submitted (At the property), any works, what happens Afterwards, the levers with their Adopt and Task buttons, and the property's own settings (strategy, extra tenants, lettable rooms, meters, council tax payer, who the tasks go to).
  4. How to raise it with a tenant: what to say, what never to say, and the honest answers when they push back. Read it before the first visit.
  5. Tenant meeting form: pick the tenant, fill it in with them, Save writes every field to the tenant's Airtable record (date of birth, NI number, contact, UC payment day, household, cap position, ID and statements seen, weekly income and spending, authority signed, meeting date and notes). Bank account numbers are never stored.
  6. Strategic order: click a row for the evidence and the first step. Adopt records it in the Growth Plan table. Task creates an Airtable task (paper and legal to Kevin, works and lettings to Roy) and links it to the row. Done and Drop take it out of the totals; "Show done and dropped" brings it back.
  7. Property cards: rooms in use against lettable rooms, each tenant's rent against their rate, and the three fields the plan needs per house.
  8. Documents: scanned ID, bank statements and screen grabs attach to the tenant's Airtable record straight from the form, up to 3.7MB each, so whoever fills in a council form has them to hand.
  9. Joint tenancies: the candidate houses, the tenancy clause and the side letter. The AI Legal and Compliance head reviewed the wording on 9 September 2026: the landlord's payment goes to the council under a side letter, never through the rent account. Templates ast_joint_template.md and council_tax_side_letter_template.md sit in Drive beside the standard AST.
  10. Finding tenants: where the last 254 applicants came from and what each channel is worth now. Above that list, Kevin sees whether the tenant chain is working (section 5).
  11. Assumptions: every figure not read from a live record. Edit here; it saves to Airtable. Update the LHA and benefit rows each April.

5. Finding tenants: the chain monitor

The tenant chain finds single adults aged 35 or over on Universal Credit for rooms that are coming free. A script runs it every morning at 08:10 (scripts/tenant-leads.py). When it finishes, it writes one report to the Estate Status table in Airtable. The box Is the tenant chain working? shows that report. It sits at the top of Finding tenants aged 35 and over on Universal Credit, under Further information. The heading carries a small label, so you see the chain's state without opening it.

Only Kevin sees the box. Roy's copy of the page does not show it or read it.

The headline

  • Green, "Working: every step on time." Nothing to do.
  • Amber, "Working, with 1 thing to watch." Something is slow or has sat open for a few days. Read its note.
  • Red, "2 steps not happening." A step that should have happened did not. The note on that row says what is missing.
  • Red, "The chain has not run since ...: nothing below is current." The script itself has stopped. The page shows this once the last run is more than 26 hours old. Nothing in the box can be trusted until it runs again.
  • Grey, "The chain has not run yet." No report exists yet.

Who does what

  • Roy gets the adverts and the viewing lists without a yes from Kevin on each task. This is Kevin's standing handover of 25 September 2026. His condition was that he can see the chain working, which is what this box is for.
  • Every email goes through Kevin's approval queue. Each mail-out is one card, sent to each person separately. Nothing leaves until Kevin approves it.
  • The £50 referral bonus lands on the Friday Payment Run list. Kevin pays it. Nothing pays it automatically.
  • Replies. Each morning the chain reads replies to its emails in info@. Anyone who replies STOP goes on the Email Opt-outs table in Airtable and is never emailed again. A NO to the check-in marks the person Not looking. A YES keeps them on the list for another 180 days.
  • Past applicants (people who applied between 2017 and 2021) are phoned by Roy, never emailed or texted, because they never agreed to be contacted about new rooms.
A red row is not a crash. It is a step that should have happened and did not. Read the note on the row first, then open the lead list or the task board.

7. Health checks

  • Portfolio tables fetched: fails on a zero count in properties, units, tenants or tenancies.
  • Settings rows present: fails under 10 rows (the page would be running on built-in defaults).
  • LHA table in date: warns after 31 March 2027.
  • Every property has a postcode: a house with no postcode gets no LHA rate.
  • Ages known for UC room tenants and Benefit cap exemptions recorded: the two facts that move the paper-trail total most.
  • Plan rows match a live lever: a Growth Plan row whose key no longer exists (tenant moved).
  • Totals reconcile: the actionable figure equals its four buckets.
  • Tenant chain ran in the last 26 hours: fails when the tenant-chain row is missing, cannot be read, or is more than 26 hours old. Kevin only: Roy's tab does not run it.
