The three strategies, and what each needs

Every self-managed house is one of these. A rent uplift follows the strategy of the house the tenant lives in.

Joint tenancy

  • Joint tenancy agreement signed (ast_joint_template.md in Drive): it stipulates the tenants are liable for council tax
  • Proof of address document (existing template)
  • Universal Credit details: name, phone, email, date of birth, payment date; change reported in the UC journal, managed payment to landlord requested
  • Council Tax Reduction form: NI number, everyone in the house, income, capital, benefits; backdated to the tenancy start; the tenant's email and a password to save it
  • Anything the form asks for on top: ID, UC statement, bank statements
  • CRF Housing Payment (Discretionary Housing) application: household income, weekly spending, two months' bank statements, payment to landlord; the shortfall from the calculator

HMO

  • Individual AST for each tenant (ast_template.md in Drive)
  • Proof of address document (existing template)
  • Universal Credit details as for a joint tenancy: name, phone, email, date of birth, payment date; journal change, managed payment
  • No Council Tax Reduction form: the owner keeps council tax on an HMO
  • CRF Housing Payment (Discretionary Housing) application where the cap bites
  • New rooms: fire-safe first (£400 a room), then let through the channels below

Leave as is

  • No change to the agreement or the household
  • Rent uplifts still apply where a tenant qualifies, using the HMO or joint tenancy list above depending on the house
  • Details still collected when you are there: date of birth, NI number, phone, email, cap position

Rent is always the full 1-bed rate; a benefit cap shortfall is met by the CRF Housing Payment, never by a lower rent. Signed authority letter (authority_to_act_template.md) from every tenant so you can chase both council claims.

How to raise it with a tenant

Nothing here costs the tenant money, and the opening line has to say so before anything else. Read the honest bits too: on a joint tenancy there is a real change in what they are signing, and hiding it is what would spook them later.

Say this

  • Open with the money. "This will not cost you anything, and it is not about arrears. Your rent is being put on the correct Universal Credit rate for your age, and Universal Credit pays the difference straight to us."
  • Why now. "The rules changed. Someone 35 or over on Universal Credit gets the one-bedroom rate even in a room. You have been on the lower rate, and you should not have been."
  • What they do. "One journal entry with me sitting next to you, and I fill in the forms. That is the whole job."
  • The cap, if it bites. "There is a cap on total benefits. If it catches you, we apply to the council for a housing payment that comes to us, not to you. You keep your standard allowance either way."
  • Joint tenancy only. "You two would go on one agreement for the whole house instead of a room each. Council tax then sits with you, you claim the reduction, and I pay anything left straight to the council."
  • Close on their gain. "You get the whole house on the agreement, the paperwork done for you, and nothing more to pay."

Do not say this

  • Never "your rent is going up" with no second half of the sentence. That is the sentence that gets a tenant on the phone to Shelter.
  • Never imply it is conditional on them signing today, or that anything happens to their tenancy if they say no.
  • Do not gloss the joint tenancy. Tell them plainly: both names on one agreement means each is liable for the whole rent if the other stops paying, and if one leaves, both sign a surrender before anything changes. Ask them if they are happy sharing with that person before you produce a pen.
  • Do not promise a CRF award. It is a council decision and short term. Say "we apply", not "you will get".
  • Do not ask for bank details or a payment. Nothing on this plan takes money from a tenant.

If they push back

  • "Will I owe anything?" — No. The housing element rises with the rent; where the cap bites we claim the shortfall from the council, paid to us.
  • "Why do you need my National Insurance number?" — The council forms will not submit without it. It goes on the forms and nowhere else.
  • "What if I say no?" — Nothing changes. Your tenancy carries on as it is.
  • "Is this a trick to raise rent later?" — The rent is the published rate for the area, set by the government each April. It is on the form we fill in together.

  • Signed authority letter so you can speak to Anglia Revenues about their claim

Submit in the same sitting

  • Universal Credit: tenant reports "change to housing costs" in the journal with the new rent and date; you request the managed payment to landlord (UC47 route) if not already in place
  • Council Tax Reduction (joint tenancies only): the Anglia Revenues "Apply for Housing Benefit and/or Council Tax Reduction" form. It asks about the tenant and everyone living with them: income, capital and other benefits; it saves against the tenant's email and a password with a reference code, so bring both. If the tenant applied for UC in the last two months and ticked Council Tax Reduction, no form is needed. When UC changes later, the "Rapid reclaim Council Tax Reduction" form (UC claimants only) is the quick route. Tell the council the liability changed.
  • CRF Housing Payment (capped tenants only): the Anglia Revenues "Apply for CRF Housing Payment and/or Exceptional Hardship Payment" form (same portal). It asks for all household income and weekly expenditure, and the last two months' bank statements; the payment option lets it go to the landlord. Awards are short term or one-off, so diarise the renewal. Take the shortfall figure from the calculator and the signed authority letter (authority_to_act_template.md in Drive).

Joint tenancies and council tax

A house let by the room is an HMO for council tax and the owner pays (from 1 December 2023). Let the whole house to two tenants on one agreement of six months or more and they become liable. They claim Council Tax Reduction from West Suffolk (run by Anglia Revenues Partnership; up to 100% for a working-age household on UC and not working); any balance left, you pay straight to the council on their account under the side letter. Their Universal Credit housing costs do not change: each joint renter keeps their own 1-bed rate up to their share of the rent. Templates: ast_joint_template.md and council_tax_side_letter_template.md in Drive, beside the standard AST.
