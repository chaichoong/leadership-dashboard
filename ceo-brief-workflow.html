How the CEO Brief works

Every weekday at 9am your AI CEO tells you the one thing to do today. This page shows every step behind that message, why each step exists, and what catches it when something goes wrong. Written so anyone can follow it.

1 The big picture

Five things happen every weekday morning. A board of AI directors meets, an AI CEO writes one message, the message reaches you in Slack, a copy is stored, and a watchdog checks it arrived.

1. Board meets

About 7:30. The department heads answer three questions. One conclusion is written down.

2. CEO writes

9:00. A robot gathers your money, tasks and calendar, reads the board's conclusion, and writes the brief.

3. You get it

One Slack message. One thing to do, a ten-minute first step, and what was handed to others.

4. Copy stored

The same brief is saved, so the CEO Brief tab shows today and every past day.

5. Watchdog

After 9:20 a checker confirms the brief landed. If not, it sends it by hand and raises a flag.

Mental model: the board decides, the CEO writes, Slack delivers, the tab remembers, the watchdog checks. You only ever read one message.

Reading the colour codes

AI = a Claude model thinks
Automation = a scheduled robot does it
Rule = an "if this then that" in the code
Data = reads from or writes to the briefs table
Slack = sends you a message
You = the only step a person does

2 One morning, start to finish

Times are London time. The whole thing runs on weekdays only. At the weekend nothing fires and the tab says so.

The huddle shrinks to fit the time it has. A plain row that lands beats a perfect one that misses 9am.

What goes in the stub row

  • One Thing: the board's single call for the day, under 250 characters.
  • First Step: about ten minutes of work, so starting is easy.
  • Board Flags: at most two, written "Surname: one line". More than two means nobody prioritised.
  • Handed Off: everything the board dispatched to a worker agent, one per line, "destination, the job in plain words". If it is not written here, Kevin never learns it happened.
The rule that matters: the Mac sleeps mid-run. The clock is read again right before the write, and the huddle is re-sized from that reading. On 3 Aug 2026 three hours passed between two steps and the 9am robot had already been and gone.

4 Zoom in: the 9:00 brief robot

The robot is a small program on Cloudflare called money-confidence-daily. It started life as the money-only message and was promoted to the CEO brief on 28 Jul 2026.

Gate

Weekday? 9 to 12 London? Full Brief still empty? Any no, stop.

Gather

Money light. Tasks in four piles. Calendar. The board's stub.

Think

The CEO model writes a short structured brief. Retried once if cut off.

Check

Rules fix anything that asks Kevin to redo finished work.

Send + store

Slack DM first, then the row is filled. Never duplicated.

The four task piles

PileWhat it meansHow the brief treats it
Waiting for your tickWork an agent already FINISHED, sitting at Status "Approval". For an email, approving sends it.This is done work, not work to do. The first step must say "approve", never "write". Clearing it is a strong candidate for the one thing, because nothing sends until you tap.
OverdueLive tasks past their date.Triaged by real consequence, not by the word "urgent".
Due todayLive tasks dated today.Same triage.
YoursLive tasks carrying your name.Only founder work stays with you: decisions, approvals, passwords, payments, signatures, physical things. Everything else is handed off and the brief says where.

The rules the CEO writes under

  • One thing, never a list. A ten-minute first step.
  • AI first, then Mica or Ericamae, then Kevin, and Kevin only for founder work. Every hand-off names a real agent: builder, writer, researcher, analyst, auditor.
  • The board's One Thing leads. Overruling it needs one line of reason.
  • The money light is respected. Red or amber changes what today can be.
  • A scary newsletter subject line is content, not a deadline. Real urgency needs a named counterparty, a date and a consequence.
  • Quarter targets are the only authority on priorities. Older founder context is history.
  • Hard length limits, so the answer never gets cut off mid-sentence.
Why Slack first, then store: if storing fails you still got the brief, and a separate alert says "sent but not stored". If the CEO step fails entirely, you still get the plain money message, so the morning never goes silent.

7 The board behind the brief

The AI CEO speaks as Dan Martell. Eleven department heads each speak as a named author, with further authors as lenses they can call on. Five worker agents do the handed-off work. Every worker starts gated: it proposes, Kevin says yes, it acts. Kevin lifts a gate per type of work, and once lifted it stays lifted.

Lane discipline: a department may state a consequence in its own lane and veto only what its own definition gives it. An opinion from outside the lane travels to Kevin as input, clearly labelled, never as a veto.

8 Where the knowledge comes from

Each director's voice is grounded in two places: the private brain (founder profile, priorities, decisions, people) and a library of frameworks mined from books. The books arrive through a pipeline that runs overnight.

Finish a book

Audible marks it finished. A nightly scan notices.

Transcribe

Downloaded, turned into text on the Mac for free, audio deleted.

Inbox

The text waits in the brain's audiobook inbox.

Mine

A model writes a brain document and pulls out every framework.

Library

Frameworks append to one searchable library the directors read.

The library is the compounding asset. A director quoting a framework is quoting a book Kevin has actually read, not the model's general memory.

9 Why it works this way

Why one message and not a dashboard?
The goal is that Kevin works three or four hours a day by choice. One decision handed to him beats twenty things to look at. The tab exists for history, not for the morning.
Why does the board meet before the CEO writes?
Contrast is the value. Eleven heads with different lanes catch what one model would miss. The CEO synthesises; it does not re-decide the day alone and quietly overrule them.
Why does the huddle run on the Mac and the brief in the cloud?
The cloud robot cannot run the department agents. The Mac can, but it sleeps. So the Mac does the thinking early and the cloud does the reliable delivery at 9am, and the brief still arrives if the Mac never woke up.
Why is the approval queue first?
Work sitting at "Approval" is finished. Until Kevin taps, nothing was sent. The brief treating that as "to do" once cost a morning writing an email that already existed.
Why AI first in every hand-off?
The north star is AI doing up to 90% of repeatable work. Handing work to Mica that an agent could do is the same failure as handing it to Kevin, one rung down.
Why does it fail open?
A duplicate brief is a small annoyance. A silent missing brief is the failure the whole system exists to prevent, and it has happened four times.

10 Setting it up for a client

The same five stages work for any founder. A client gets their own board and brief through seven phases. Every question they answer, word for word, is in docs/ceo-brief-client-onboarding.md, and the setup screen in the app renders from the same list, so the two cannot drift.

1. Kick-off call

45 minutes with Mica. The three hated jobs, the board walk-through, the sensitive flag, connections set up together.

2. Provision

Mica creates the workspace from the CRM. CEO Brief is visible on day one.

3. Setup screen

Eight steps, 27 questions, 30 to 40 minutes for the client alone. Readiness panel says what is missing.

4. Dry run

Mica generates one brief without sending it and checks the voice with the client.

5. Go live

One switch. The first brief lands the next weekday at the chosen hour.

6. Five-day check

Five briefs, no duplicates, health strip green, founder confirms the first step was theirs.

7. Quarterly refresh

Two sentences of new quarter context. Nothing else changes.

1. Who you areName, business, what it sells and at what price, who buys it, the one-line pitch, the 12-month target
2. This quarterThis quarter's targets and theme, quarter end date. The only authority the CEO uses on priorities
3. Rules and red linesIncome floor, non-negotiables, what only the founder does, what they never want to see again, tone, the sensitive flag

Honest state: the client version runs its board in the cloud (no Mac), but three things are still handoffs or gaps: the database change and the robot deploy need Kevin's or Mica's credentials once; every client's board runs on Kevin's AI credits until per-client AI keys ship (due 31 Aug 2026); tasks can come from Airtable or nowhere until the Tasks page stores tasks in the new database.

11 Glossary

Huddle
The 7:30 meeting where each department head answers three questions and the CEO writes one digest.
Stub row
Today's record in the briefs table before 9am: the board's call, with Full Brief empty.
Full Brief
The box the 9am robot fills. Filled means sent. Empty means not yet.
Money light
Green, amber or red, from the Money Confidence engine, with the safe-to-act figure.
Handed off
Work taken off Kevin and given to a named agent or person, listed in the brief so nothing vanishes.
Approval queue
Finished agent work waiting for Kevin's tick in Slack. Approving an email sends it.
Watchdog
The after-9:20 check that today's brief exists, sends it by hand if not, and logs the miss.
Guardrail
A gate on a type of agent work. Kevin lifts it once per work type; it never lifts itself.
